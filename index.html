Get progress. Improve what matters.

A few examples of the kind of work we can do. The common thread is not the technology. It is finding a painful problem and making useful progress with the people who own the work.

HR · Payroll data · 4 countries

From 2–3 days of monthly work to about 1 hour

Problem: External payroll agencies supplied large, inconsistent Excel files. Data had different naming conventions, cost-centre structures and country-specific requirements. HR had to prepare payroll inputs and reports manually.

What we did: Built country-specific automated Excel tools, matched agency data with employee data and KPIs, mapped costs to cost centres and generated reports. We iterated with the HR user and trained her on the final version.

Result: the process went from roughly 2–3 days to about 1–3 hours per month, with the HR user moving from copy-and-paste work to checking and controlling the agency data.
Operations · Parcel delivery

A live truck-to-ramp scheduler built around the real operation

Problem: The existing system assigned shipments to trucks, but not trucks to loading ramps. People planned the operation manually on paper and in Excel.

What we did: Built and iterated an interactive scheduler. It pulled fresh system data, assigned trucks to ramps according to operational rules and displayed timeslots, bookings and key truck KPIs on a live master view.

Result: around 20 people used the tool. They could see arrivals, empty capacity and upcoming work at a glance instead of coordinating the operation manually.
Logistics · Cost & pricing

A slow cost calculator became an instant decision tool

Problem: A logistics provider had an Access-based calculator that was too slow for practical use. It calculated shipment costs across multiple countries, warehouses, handling stages and routing options.

What we did: Rebuilt the calculator in Excel, iterated it with the analyst, then extended it to generate pricing lists from shipment addresses and different shipment sizes. We also worked on margin calculations.

Result: cost calculations became effectively instant and the tool was used across multiple European entities.
Data · Reporting · Multiple functions

Turning disconnected data into reports people could actually use

Problem: Important data existed in different systems and files, but teams could not easily see the combined picture.

What we did: Cleaned and integrated data, built practical reporting tools and, where appropriate, added recommendations based on what the combined data revealed.

Result: teams gained views of sales, products, regions, invoices, spending and other performance information that had previously been difficult or impossible to produce.

These are deliberately practical examples: not every project needs a new system. Sometimes a well-designed Excel tool is the fastest route to a better process.
